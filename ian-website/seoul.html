Discovering Seoul

A City of Old and New

Seoul is the capital of South Korea and one of the largest cities in Asia. Roughly 9.4 million people live in the city itself, and the wider capital region is home to about half of the country's population. The Han River runs through the middle of the city from east to west, splitting it into a northern half, where the historic center and royal palaces are, and a southern half, known for neighborhoods like Gangnam.

What I love most about Seoul is how easily the old and the new sit side by side. You can walk through Gyeongbokgung, a palace first built in the 14th century during the Joseon dynasty, and ten minutes later be surrounded by glass office towers, neon signs and some of the fastest internet in the world. Traditional hanok houses in Bukchon sit on the same hills as modern apartment blocks, and street food stalls serve tteokbokki and hotteok just outside high-end department stores.

Seoul also has four very distinct seasons. Spring brings cherry blossoms along the river, summer is hot, humid and rainy during the monsoon, autumn turns the mountains red and gold, and winter is cold and dry with temperatures often below freezing. Places I Recommend Visiting

  1. Gyeongbokgung Palace: the largest of Seoul's royal palaces, especially beautiful in autumn.
  2. Bukchon Hanok Village: a neighborhood of traditional Korean houses between two palaces.
  3. N Seoul Tower: the best view of the city, at the top of Namsan mountain.
  4. Han River parks: perfect for biking, picnics and night views of the bridges.
  5. Gwangjang Market: one of the oldest markets in Seoul, famous for its street food.
  6. Myeongdong: a busy shopping street full of cosmetics stores and food stalls.
